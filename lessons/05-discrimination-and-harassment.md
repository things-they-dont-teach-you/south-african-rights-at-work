# Lesson 5: Discrimination and harassment
<!-- facts: Dispute time limits; Earnings threshold; Where to get help -->

## By the end, the learner can

- Explain unfair discrimination, and the difference between direct and indirect discrimination.
- Say when treating people differently is fair.
- Recognise sexual harassment, racial harassment and bullying linked to discrimination.
- List the steps to take if it happens to them, and the time limit (from `facts.md`).

## Key ideas

**Unfair discrimination is banned** in any employment policy or practice, by any person, on
grounds including race, gender, sex, pregnancy, marital status, family responsibility, ethnic
or social origin, colour, sexual orientation, age, disability, religion, HIV status, conscience,
belief, political opinion, culture, language, birth, or any other arbitrary ground (EEA section
6). It protects job applicants too.

**Direct** discrimination treats you worse because of one of those grounds ("we don't hire
women for this"). **Indirect** discrimination is a rule that looks neutral but hits one group
harder for no good reason (a height rule that excludes most women, for a job that doesn't need
height).

**When different treatment is fair:** affirmative action measures under the EEA, or an inherent
requirement of the job (a sign language interpreter must know sign language). Equal pay applies
to work of equal value.

**Harassment is a form of unfair discrimination** (EEA section 6(3)). The 2022 Code of Good
Practice on harassment describes it as unwanted conduct that impairs dignity and creates a
hostile work environment. Examples:
- **Sexual harassment:** unwanted touching, sexual comments, jokes or messages, or "sleep with
  me or lose your job" (quid pro quo). One serious incident can be enough.
- **Racial or ethnic harassment:** racist names, jokes, memes or exclusion.
- **Bullying** linked to a ground of discrimination: constant humiliation, isolation, setting
  someone up to fail.

**Your employer must act:** protect workers from harassment by colleagues, clients or anyone
else at work, have a clear complaints process, and handle complaints confidentially.

**What you can do:**
1. Write down every incident: date, time, what happened, witnesses. Keep messages.
2. Tell the harasser to stop, if it's safe to.
3. Report it: to your shop steward if you're in a union, otherwise HR or a manager. In a very
   small business, contact the CCMA.
4. If it isn't resolved, refer it to the CCMA within the time limit (`facts.md`). After
   conciliation, sexual harassment cases can go to CCMA arbitration whatever you earn; other
   discrimination cases can if you earn up to the earnings threshold, otherwise to the Labour
   Court.

Assault or sexual assault is also a crime: report it to the police (`facts.md`).

## Worked example

A supervisor keeps sending Naledi sexual messages and hints her contract won't be renewed if
she complains. That is sexual harassment and a threat linked to it. She saves the messages,
reports it to HR, and notes that her six-month CCMA time limit runs from the incidents.

## Exercise

Ask the learner to sort these: "only Christians may apply" for a bank teller job; a church
hiring a minister of its own faith; a manager who mocks one worker's accent every day. Which are
unfair discrimination or harassment, and why?
