# Lesson 2: Minimum wage, hours and overtime
<!-- facts: National minimum wage; Earnings threshold; Working hours and overtime -->

## By the end, the learner can

- Check whether a wage meets the national minimum wage (from `facts.md`).
- Say the limits on ordinary hours and overtime, and what overtime, Sunday and public holiday
  work must pay.
- Explain who the hours rules protect, using the earnings threshold.
- Say where to report being paid too little.

## Key ideas

**The national minimum wage** is the lowest hourly rate almost any worker may be paid, including
farm and domestic workers (`facts.md`). It goes up each year, usually from 1 March. Some sectors
have higher minimums. An employer may not cut your pay, hours or benefits on their own to cover
a minimum wage increase.

**Ordinary hours** have a weekly and a daily limit (`facts.md`). Your contract can't make you
work more as ordinary time.

**Overtime** needs your agreement, has its own daily and weekly limits, and pays more than normal
time, or you get paid time off instead if you agree (`facts.md`).

**Sundays and public holidays** pay extra if you work them, and you can only be made to work a
public holiday by agreement. If a public holiday falls on a day you normally work and you stay
home, you still get your normal pay.

**Breaks.** After 5 hours of continuous work you get a meal break of at least an hour (a written
agreement can cut it to 30 minutes). You also get a daily rest of 12 hours between shifts and a
weekly rest of 36 hours (BCEA sections 14 and 15).

**Who these rules protect.** The hours, overtime and break rules apply to people earning up to
the earnings threshold (`facts.md`). Above it, your contract sets them. The minimum wage, leave
and dismissal rules apply to everyone.

**Paid too little?** Report it at your nearest labour centre, or refer it to the CCMA if you
earn up to the earnings threshold (lesson 8). Choose one, not both.

## Worked example

Johannes is a domestic worker who works 8 hours a day, 5 days a week.

1. That is 40 hours a week, within the ordinary-hours limit.
2. At the minimum hourly rate in `facts.md`, his weekly minimum is 40 × that rate. Ask the
   learner to work it out: 40 × R30.23 = R1 209.20.
3. If he works 2 extra hours one Saturday, that is overtime, paid at at least 1.5 times his
   hourly rate: 2 × 1.5 × R30.23 = R90.69.

## Exercise

Ask the learner: a shop worker earns R5 500 a month for 45 hours a week. Is that at least the
national minimum? (A month is about 4.33 weeks.) Is a 13-hour shift with no overtime agreement
allowed? Use `facts.md`.
