# Lesson 1: Your contract and your payslip
<!-- facts: UIF contributions; Earnings threshold -->

## By the end, the learner can

- Say what an employer must tell them in writing when they start a job.
- Read a payslip and say what each deduction is, and which deductions are allowed.
- Explain what UIF is and how much comes off their pay (from `facts.md`).
- Say why it matters whether they are an employee or an independent contractor.

## Key ideas

**Which laws protect you.** The Constitution gives everyone the right to fair labour practices.
The main Acts that put this into practice are the Basic Conditions of Employment Act (BCEA: pay,
hours, leave, notice), the Labour Relations Act (LRA: dismissal, unions, the CCMA), the
Employment Equity Act (EEA: discrimination, harassment) and the Occupational Health and Safety
Act (OHSA). The basic rights can't be signed away in a contract.

**Written particulars.** When you start, your employer must give you in writing: their name and
address, your job, where you work, start date, hours and days, wage and how it is worked out,
overtime rate, deductions, leave, notice period, and any bargaining council or sectoral
determination that covers the business (BCEA section 29). Ask for it if you didn't get it.

**Your payslip.** Each payday you must get, in writing: the period paid for, your pay, the amount
and reason for every deduction, what you actually received, and (if relevant) your hourly rate
and the ordinary, overtime, Sunday and public holiday hours worked (BCEA section 33).

**Deductions.** An employer may only deduct money if a law, court order or collective agreement
allows it (PAYE, UIF), or you agreed in writing to a specific debt. For loss or damage you caused,
the employer must first follow a fair procedure, and can take at most a quarter of your pay in
money (BCEA section 34).

**UIF.** You and your employer each pay a small share of your pay into the Unemployment Insurance
Fund, up to a ceiling (`facts.md`). It pays you if you lose your job or take maternity, parental
or illness leave (lesson 7). Check UIF actually appears on your payslip.

**PAYE** is income tax your employer takes for SARS. The south-african-tax course covers it.

**Employee or contractor?** Most rights in this course are for employees. Calling you a
"contractor" or "freelancer" doesn't settle it: what matters is how you actually work. If you
earn at or below the earnings threshold (`facts.md`), the LRA presumes you're an employee if, for
example, someone controls how and when you work, you work only for them, or they supply your
tools. Domestic workers are employees. Gig and platform workers are often treated as
contractors; whether that is right depends on the facts, so ask the CCMA.

## Worked example

Lindiwe is a cashier. Her payslip shows gross pay, PAYE, UIF and "Till shortage R400". UIF and
PAYE are allowed by law. The till shortage is only allowed if she agreed in writing, the shop
held a fair process first, and it is no more than the actual loss and a quarter of her pay.

## Exercise

Ask the learner to list what a payslip must show, then spot what is missing from an example the
tutor makes up (for example, no overtime hours, or a deduction with no reason). Remind them not
to share their real payslip.
