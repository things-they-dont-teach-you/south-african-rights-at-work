# Lesson 6: Health and safety at work
<!-- facts: Where to get help -->

## By the end, the learner can

- Say what an employer must do to keep the workplace safe.
- Say what the worker's own duties are.
- Explain why they can't be charged for safety equipment, or punished for reporting danger.
- Say where to turn after a workplace injury or illness.

## Key ideas

**The employer's duty.** Under the Occupational Health and Safety Act (OHSA section 8), the
employer must provide and maintain, as far as reasonably practicable, a workplace that is safe
and without risk to health. That includes safe machines and systems of work, finding hazards
and dealing with them, training and supervision, and enforcing safety measures.

**Your duties** (OHSA section 14): take reasonable care of your own and others' safety; follow
lawful safety rules and procedures; report anything unsafe to your employer or health and
safety representative as soon as you can; and report any incident in which you were injured.

**Safety equipment is free.** An employer may not deduct money or make you pay for anything the
law requires them to provide for your safety, such as protective clothing. They can recover the
cost only if you deliberately damaged it, after a proper investigation (OHSA section 23).

**Reporting is protected.** An employer may not dismiss you, cut your pay or treat you worse
because you gave information about safety to an inspector or another official (OHSA section 26).

**Health and safety representatives.** Workplaces with more than 20 employees must have them.
They inspect, raise hazards with the employer and can be your first stop.

**Injured or made ill by work?** Report it to your employer straight away and get medical help.
The Compensation for Occupational Injuries and Diseases Act (COIDA) can pay medical costs and
compensation, without you having to prove the employer was at fault. Domestic workers have been
covered since a 2020 Constitutional Court ruling. If your employer won't report it, go to your
nearest labour centre.

**Unsafe and nobody acts?** Contact a labour centre; inspectors can inspect and order changes.

## Worked example

Bongani works in a warehouse. His employer says he must buy his own safety boots, R450 off his
pay. That breaks OHSA section 23: required safety equipment must be provided free. He can raise
it with the health and safety rep or a labour centre.

## Exercise

Ask the learner to list three hazards in a workplace they know (a kitchen, a taxi, a shop),
then say who should fix each one and what the worker should do about it.
