# Lesson 3: Leave: annual, sick, family and parental
<!-- facts: Leave; UIF benefits -->

## By the end, the learner can

- Work out their minimum annual leave and sick leave (from `facts.md`).
- Say when an employer can ask for a sick note.
- Say when family responsibility leave applies.
- Explain parental leave after the 2025 Constitutional Court ruling, and where pay comes from.

## Key ideas

**Who gets leave.** The leave rules apply if you work at least 24 hours a month for the
employer, whatever you earn. Your contract or a bargaining council can give more, never less.

**Annual leave** is paid holiday (`facts.md`). Your employer must let you take it within six
months of the end of each leave year, and may not pay you instead of letting you take it, except
for leave still owed when you leave the job. If a public holiday falls during your leave, you
get an extra day.

**Sick leave** works in a three-year cycle, not per year (`facts.md`). In your first six months
you build it up slowly. Your employer can ask for a medical certificate in some cases (`facts.md`), from
a doctor or another health practitioner registered with a professional council.

**Family responsibility leave** covers a sick child or a death in the close family (`facts.md`).
Since the 2018 law changes, the birth of a child is covered by parental leave instead.

**Parental leave.** In October 2025 the Constitutional Court ruled that the old rules (four
months' maternity leave for birth mothers, only ten days for other parents) unfairly
discriminated. Until Parliament changes the BCEA, its interim order applies (`facts.md`): a
single parent, or the only employed parent, gets at least four months; two employed parents
share four months and ten days between them, as they agree. A birth mother may still not work
for six weeks after the birth unless a doctor or midwife says she is fit to.

**Who pays during maternity or parental leave.** The law makes the employer give the leave, not
pay it. Unless your contract says otherwise, you claim a UIF benefit (lesson 7). Being dismissed
because you are pregnant is automatically unfair (lesson 4).

## Worked example

Ayanda works five days a week. Each year she gets at least 15 working days' annual leave (21
consecutive days). Over each three-year sick leave cycle she gets 30 working days of paid sick
leave (six weeks × five days), however they are spread.

## Exercise

Ask the learner: someone works six days a week. How many paid sick days do they get per
three-year cycle? (36.) Their child has flu: which leave applies, and after how long in the job?
