# Your rights at work in South Africa

What the law gives you as a worker in South Africa, taught by your AI assistant: your contract
and payslip, minimum wage and hours, leave, dismissal, discrimination and harassment, safety,
claiming UIF, and taking a dispute to the CCMA.

## This is not legal advice

This course explains how labour law works. It can't tell you whether you have a case. For your
own situation, ask the [CCMA](https://www.ccma.org.za) (free), your union,
[Legal Aid South Africa](https://legal-aid.co.za) or a labour lawyer. If you were dismissed,
don't wait: the time limit to go to the CCMA is short (see [facts.md](facts.md)).

## Known gaps

Things this course could not yet verify from an official source, or leaves out. Help welcome.

- **Earnings threshold.** The gazette (GN 7384, 17 April 2026) says R269 600.90 a year; the
  Department's media statement of 21 April 2026 says R269 900.90. The course uses the gazette.
- **Parental leave.** The course follows the Constitutional Court's interim order in *Van Wyk*
  (3 October 2025), read from the judgment's reading-in text. Not yet checked: whether
  Parliament has since amended the BCEA, and how UIF now pays parental benefits when parents
  share the leave.
- **No official up-to-date text of the Acts.** The BCEA copy on labour.gov.za predates the 2002
  and 2018 amendments (for example, notice periods and family responsibility leave), so the
  course reads the Acts together with their amending Acts on gov.za. A consolidated official
  text would be better.
- **UIF coverage.** The UIF basic guide's list of excluded workers (public servants, learners,
  foreign contract workers) looks out of date after the 2016 amendments. The course only states
  the 24-hours-a-month rule.
- **Who counts as an employee.** The LRA section 200A presumption is summarised, not quoted. The
  status of gig and platform workers is unsettled, and no official guidance was found.
- **COIDA.** How to claim for a work injury, and the time limits, are not covered yet.
- **Sector rules.** Bargaining council agreements and sectoral determinations (for example for
  domestic, farm, security and hospitality workers) can give more; the course only flags this.
- **Strikes.** The procedure for a protected strike is mentioned, not taught.
- **Contacts.** The UIF toll-free number comes from an undated UIF guide. Legal Aid South
  Africa's advice line hours, and what it can do at the CCMA, were not confirmed on its own site.
  No current Department of Employment and Labour call centre number was confirmed, so the
  course points to labour centres.
- **CCMA sources.** Some CCMA info sheets are from 2018 and 2020, and the CCMA website blocks
  some automated checks, so a newer version may exist.
